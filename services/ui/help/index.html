Getting started

Drone Mission Cloud is one platform with two sides: the manufacturer runs its operations, and each customer runs its own fleet. The hosted demo plays a scripted sortie on a real range in California's central coast, with nine vehicles in the air and three on the pad.

  1. Open the console and sign in with the account you were given (see demo accounts).
  2. A customer account opens on Operate. A manufacturer account opens on the Work queue.
  3. Press ⌘K (or Ctrl K) to jump to any vehicle, page or filter. Press ? for the shortcut sheet.
  4. Take an action, then sign out and sign in as the other side to see its effect. Demo state lives in your browser tab, so it carries across accounts. Reloading starts the scenario over.
Every page shows UNCLASSIFIED // DEMO DATA in the banner, and every simulated figure carries a SIM label.

The two workspaces

Your organisation decides your workspace, and your role shapes the menu on the left: each role lands on its own home and sees the pages it works in, in the order its work runs. Every other page in the workspace is still one ⌘K away. A red count beside a page means something there is waiting on you.

Manufacturer workspace

Work queue, then Deliver (Contract to field, Program), Make (Quality, Compliance, Configuration, Software updates), Sell (Quotes, Surge planner, Bid desk, Pricing plans, Releasability) and Learn (Field fixes, Attrition, Field reports, Evidence, Loadout planner). The manufacturer never sees a customer's people or another customer's fleet, and sees loss data only while that customer shares it.

Customer workspace

Fly (Operate, Brief, Missions, Debrief, Loadout planner, GNSS denial map), Fleet (Airframes, All vendors, Configuration, Stockpile health, Crew readiness), Supply (Receiving, Accountability, Requests to Neros, Order, Software updates) and Learn (Attrition, Field reports). Operators see the flying pages; maintainers start on Airframes and see the fleet and supply pages first. The manufacturer's internals return a refusal, not an empty page.

What flows between them

  • Requests from the customer: airframes, spares, training and repairs.
  • Shipments from the maker, each with its birth record and packet.
  • Loss data to the maker, only while the customer has sharing on.

What is simulated

Read every number by its label:

  • PX4 SITL PX4 v1.18 flies in SIH with its own EKF2, and the onboard code closes the loop over MAVLink.
  • Unit check The onboard C module runs open loop against a synthetic or recorded stream.
  • SIM Demo scenario data: fleets, customers, orders, prices, lots, losses and training records.

Manufacturer catalogue items appear as published. Other vendors appear under generic labels, never real product names. Prices are planning assumptions, not anyone's price list.

The Operate screen

One screen for one operator and many vehicles. Left to right: the vehicle list, the tactical picture, the flight display for the selected vehicle, and the event timeline along the bottom.

  • The list sorts by need. Vehicles that need you rise to the top with the reason: link lost, GNSS rejected by the spoof gate, low battery, grounded.
  • The map works air-gapped. Terrain is baked offline. Overlays show survey coverage, the jamming zone, each VIO error circle, link quality and the current task allocation. Every overlay is labelled SIM.
  • 3D shows the same picture in relief, each vehicle at its height above ground and the jamming zone as a dome (heights ×2).
  • Replay. Drag the timeline, or use ← →, to see what the console received at any moment.
  • Field report. Select a vehicle and press R to file what you saw. Reports feed the fleet's record.

Add ?fleet=300 to the console URL for the fleet-scale view.

Launch readiness

Press L to see the vehicles on the ground. Each gets a go or no-go from four checks, and the row shows the first one that fails, so you know what to fix without opening anything:

  1. A fresh link.
  2. Airworthiness from the fleet record (a failed end-of-line test or an open work order fails it).
  3. Battery at or above 60%.
  4. A position fix: trusted GNSS, or VIO.

The console times each vehicle from power-on to GO and keeps the median. In the demo, NX-12 stays NO-GO because its bench test failed, and NX-11 sits at 41% battery.

GO is advice. The console does not arm or launch anything.

Jamming, VIO and lost link

GNSS rejected. When the spoof gate rejects GNSS, the vehicle navigates on visual-inertial odometry. The map draws its position error as a circle that grows with distance flown (an assumed 1% in the demo). NX-04 orbits inside the jamming zone to show it.

Lost link. A vehicle that stops reporting goes stale after 20 seconds and rises to the top of the list. Its tasks go back to the fleet, and the remaining vehicles re-plan with CBBA. NX-07 drops off the link about every 100 seconds in the demo.

Denial map. The Denial map page builds a picture of where GNSS was denied or rejected from repeated passes, so the next plan can route around it.

FlyCommander

Brief and two-person approval

  1. On Brief, type the mission in plain words, for example "Search the north field with two vehicles before 14:00, then check the culvert, keep two in reserve".
  2. The console proposes a plan: areas, vehicles, reserves, and which vehicles it left out and why (low battery, grounded, on hold).
  3. A planner or mission commander approves it. The person who wrote the brief cannot approve their own plan.

The parser is a fixed grammar that runs offline. There is no language model in the loop, so the same words always give the same plan.

Operators can read a Brief but cannot approve one. The refusal is written to the audit log.

RecordMaintainer

Airframes and birth records

Every airframe has a birth record from the end-of-line bench: its serial, the lot of every major part, its calibrations, its firmware hash and the bench test result. The record decides airworthiness, so a failed hover test grounds the serial before it ships.

Field events attach to the same serial: receiving, sorties, swaps, reports, losses. That is what lets the attrition autopsy find a bad lot.

RecordMaintainer

Quality holds and work orders

A lot hold restricts every tail that carries the lot. An open work order blocks return to service. To clear a vehicle:

  1. Open Airframes and pick the restricted tail.
  2. Close its open work orders. Parts come from the maker's depot stock.
  3. Return it to service. Launch readiness picks up the change at once.

Closing a work order needs the maintainer, mission commander or admin role.

Bid desk and surge

Bid desk answers each requirement of a solicitation from the record. Every "met" row links to the evidence; rows outside the platform's scope (anything weapons-related, per ADR 0016) say so.

Surge planner shows the date a surge order can ship. The plain date never beats the slowest part; expediting and a second shift are never later and show what they cost.

Attrition and the fix loop

Attrition ranks lots by their loss rate against the fleet baseline and proposes a quality hold on the tails that carry a flagged lot. In the demo, ESC lot ESC-2611 loses airframes at 2.14× the expected rate.

Field fixes tracks the corrective change. A case closes only when the treated unit improves against the control units by a measured margin (a difference in differences with z ≤ −1.28 and at least 40 sorties a side). A change that does nothing never closes.

Trust

What the console will never do

The platform has no weapon, payload-release or fire-control type in its data model (ADR 0016). Strike airframes appear only as catalogue items.
  • It never arms, launches or commands a vehicle. It reads MAVLink and sends no MAVLink commands.
  • It accepts no commands from external systems. Feeds to TAK and other C2 systems go out only; an inbound command is refused and audited.
  • Outcomes are neutral: completed, aborted, lost. There are no target, strike or damage fields.
  • Tracking means an operator-designated object, in simulation, with confirm, abort and timeout written to the audit log.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How do I reset the demo?

Reload the page. The scenario starts over; your sign-in lasts 12 hours.

Why did an action say "role_denied"?

Your role cannot do it. That is the point of the demo: sign in as the role that can, and the refusal stays in the audit log.

Does it work on a phone?

Yes for reading and acting on queues. The Operate screen is built for a laptop or a ground-station display.

Is this a Neros product?

No. It is an independent concept by Kevin Owens. Manufacturer catalogue items appear as published; everything else is simulated.

Where is the API?

See the developer site, which has a live API explorer that runs against the demo.
