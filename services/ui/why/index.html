What I saw

Neros is going from prototype to volume, and the product around the drone has to keep up.

Your open roles tell the story: supply planning, MES with serialization and genealogy, supplier quality, lithium logistics, a ground station for many vehicles, autonomy evaluation. That is a company building at volume for units that lose airframes every day.

At the same time, programs are asking for open architectures and furnishing their own control software, units buy by the package from a marketplace, and airframes are reconfigured in the field. Whoever owns the ground screen no longer owns the customer. What stays valuable is the record of each serial, from the bench to its last sortie, and the sale that the record makes easier.

The product calls

Six decisions, and why I made them.

  1. One record per serial, not six systems. The bench, shipping, flight, loss autopsy and the lot hold all attach to the same airframe, so a 2× loss rate on one ESC lot turns into a hold and a measured fix, not a spreadsheet hunt.
  2. Two sides on one platform. Neros works a queue that ends in actions; each customer sees its own fleet and nothing else. Field data flows back only while the customer shares it, because that is the only way units will agree to share it.
  3. Exceptions first, humans approve. One operator watching dozens of drones needs the ones that need them, with the reason. Plans need a second approver. The console advises and never arms or fires: targeting and fire control stay on the airframe.
  4. Evidence labeled by where it was measured. PX4 SITL, unit check or simulation, on every number. Nothing here is presented as a field result, and the open problems are on the front page.
  5. Priced into the drone. The record travels with the airframe, so it is priced with it. Fleets pay per formation, so more drones never means more friction. These are assumptions to test with buyers, not a price list.
  6. Allies on the same platform. An allied tenant gets its own workspace, and withheld fields are removed at the API, not hidden on the screen.

What I'd do first

My first 90 days.

DAYS 1–30

Listen where the drones are

Ride along with operators and trainers, walk the line in Torrance and Tennessee, and map where today's record breaks between the bench, the shipment and the field.

DAYS 31–60

Pick one loop and ship it

Choose the loop with the most money in it, probably field losses back to a lot hold, and get it running with one unit and one production line.

DAYS 61–90

A roadmap tied to programs

A roadmap the program leads can sell from, one north-star metric (effective sorties per operator-hour), and the demo configurations for the next fly-off.

How it was built

Built in days, with the decisions in the open.

I built this in a few days with AI coding agents doing most of the typing, while I made the product calls: what to build, what to cut, and what to say honestly about. The source, the architecture decision records and every pull request are public on GitHub. Neros has not seen or endorsed any of it, and none of its data is in it: the catalog items are as Neros publishes them, and everything else is simulated.
